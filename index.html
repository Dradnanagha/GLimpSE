How the simulator works

You set a patient's features. The tool first estimates the trial-style benefit using effect sizes from large published trials (for example SURMOUNT-5 and SURPASS-2). It then applies a real-world adjustment: real-world glucose and weight results are usually well below trial figures, and some people stop the medicine, which lowers the average benefit further.

The medicine effects are real published averages; everything else (the individual "patients") is synthetic — generated by simple formulas, not taken from real records. So the tool teaches the shape of the trial-to-real-world gap; it cannot predict any real person's outcome.

About this tool

This is a teaching demonstration of why a medicine's headline number overstates the benefit a whole population gets, once side effects and early stopping are counted. It pairs the simulation with a real analysis of FDA side-effect reports for the same drug class.

It is built for early medical and nursing students and anyone curious, in plain language. It is not a medical device and not for patient care.
